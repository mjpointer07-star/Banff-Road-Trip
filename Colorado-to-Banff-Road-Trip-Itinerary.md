## Part 1: Drive Up — Colorado to Glacier National Park

**Total distance:** ~1,000 miles / ~14 hours driving  
**Driving straight through on June 19.** Leave early morning, arrive Glacier area by evening.

### Tips for the Long Drive

## Part 2: Glacier National Park — 1.5 Days (June 20–21 morning)

### June 20 (Full Day) — Going-to-the-Sun Road & West Side

**Morning:**
- Enter from the West Entrance (Apgar/West Glacier)
- Drive **Going-to-the-Sun Road** — 50 miles of stunning mountain scenery
- Stop at **Logan Pass** (6,646 ft) if road is fully open
  - Hike **Hidden Lake Overlook** (3mi RT, boardwalk, mountain goats, possible snow patches)

**Afternoon:**
- **Trail of the Cedars** — Short flat boardwalk through ancient cedar/hemlock forest
- **Avalanche Lake** (4.5mi RT, moderate) — Glacier-fed lake surrounded by waterfalls
- Stop at pullouts along Going-to-the-Sun Road for photos of Bird Woman Falls, Weeping Wall

**Evening:**
- Dinner in West Glacier or Apgar Village
- Sunset at **Lake McDonald** — mirror-calm water with colorful rocks on the shore

### June 21 (Half Day, Morning) — Many Glacier or East Side

**Morning:**
- Drive to Many Glacier area (~1.5 hrs from West Glacier, going around the south side)
- Hike **Redrock Falls** (3.6mi RT, easy/moderate) — Best option for a half day
  - *Alternative:* **St. Mary Falls + Virginia Falls** (3.6mi RT) from Going-to-the-Sun Road east side
- Watch for grizzlies, moose, and mountain goats — Many Glacier is prime wildlife territory
- **Boat tour on Swiftcurrent Lake & Lake Josephine** if time allows (~1.5 hrs)

**Afternoon:**
- Head toward the Canadian border by early afternoon
- Drive to Waterton / Banff (see Part 3)

### Glacier NP — Practical Info
- **NO vehicle reservation required in 2026** — Just drive in!
- **Entrance fee:** $35/vehicle (7-day pass) or use America the Beautiful Pass ($80/year)
- **Logan Pass parking:** No time limit for your dates (3-hour limit starts July 1)
- **Bear spray:** Required on hikes, buy in gateway towns
- **Snow on trails:** Likely at higher elevations in late June — bring microspikes/traction
- **Gas:** Fill up before entering the park — no gas stations inside
- **Weather:** Can change rapidly; pack rain layers even on sunny mornings

## Part 3: Glacier to Banff — Scenic Drive with Stops

### Route: East Glacier → Chief Mountain Border Crossing → Waterton → Crowsnest Pass → Banff

**Total drive time:** ~5–6 hours with stops

### Stop 1: Waterton Lakes National Park (Half-Day)

Right across the border from Glacier — its Canadian sister park. Smaller, fewer crowds, stunning.

- **Cameron Falls** — 2-minute walk from town, vivid blue-green water
- **Red Rock Canyon** — Short drive, beautiful red rock gorge with easy walking trails
- **Waterton Shoreline Cruise** — Boat ride down Upper Waterton Lake (crosses into the US)
- **Prince of Wales Hotel viewpoint** — Iconic hilltop view over the lake and mountains
- Grab lunch in Waterton townsite

### Stop 2: Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump (UNESCO World Heritage Site)

- Near Fort Macleod, AB
- Cliff where Indigenous peoples drove bison for over 6,000 years
- Excellent interpretive centre built into the cliff face
- Worth 1–2 hours if history/culture interests you

### Stop 3: Frank Slide (Crowsnest Pass)

- In 1903, 110 million tonnes of rock buried part of the town of Frank
- The debris field is massive and eerie — visible from the highway
- Frank Slide Interpretive Centre tells the full story (~1 hour visit)

### Stop 4: Crowsnest Pass to Banff

- Mountain scenery returns as you re-enter the Rockies
- Drive through Canmore (just outside Banff) — good spot to grab dinner and settle in

### Border Crossing Tips
- **Chief Mountain Border Crossing** — Seasonal (usually open mid-May to Sept). Confirm open before you go. Shorter lines than Carway.
- **Carway/Piegan** — Year-round alternative if Chief Mountain is closed
- Have passports ready
- Declare all food, alcohol, and outdoor gear
- **Leave US bear spray behind** — restricted at the border. Buy new cans in Waterton or Banff

## Part 4: Banff & Area — 5 Days (June 22–26)

### June 22 (Day 1) — Banff Town & Surroundings

**Morning:**
- **Banff Gondola** up Sulphur Mountain — go early (first car up at 8am) to beat crowds
- Walk the summit boardwalk trail between peaks, 360° panoramic views

**Afternoon:**
- **Bow Falls** — Easy walk from downtown, powerful cascade
- Stroll the Bow River trail
- Browse Banff Avenue shops

**Evening:**
- **Banff Upper Hot Springs** — Outdoor mineral pool at 1,585m elevation with mountain views
- Dinner: Park Distillery (campfire-style), The Bison (local/wild game), or Magpie & Stump (Tex-Mex, patio)

---

### June 23 (Day 2) — ✅ RESERVED: Lake Louise, Moraine Lake & Castle Mountain Shuttle Tour

**Shuttle tour booked for this date.**

**Early Morning:**
- Catch your shuttle (get the earliest departure for best light — before 10am ideal)
- **Moraine Lake** — Hike the Rockpile Trail for the classic viewpoint, walk the lakeshore
- Take the connector shuttle to Lake Louise

**Late Morning / Afternoon:**
- **Lake Louise** — Walk the flat shoreline trail to the back of the lake (Victoria Glacier views)
- Optional: Hike to Lake Agnes Tea House (7km RT, moderate) or canoe on the lake
- **Castle Mountain** drive-by — Iconic flat-topped mountain along Hwy 1A (Bow Valley Parkway)

**Tips for this day:**
- Morning light is best for photography (clouds build in the afternoon)
- Bring layers — shuttle wait areas can be cool early morning
- Pack snacks/lunch — limited food options at the lakes

**Evening:**
- Dinner at Lake Louise Station Restaurant or head back to Banff

---

### June 24 (Day 3) — Icefields Parkway Day Trip

**The Drive:**
- Highway 93N from Lake Louise toward Jasper — widely considered one of the most beautiful drives in the world
- 230km of glaciers, turquoise lakes, waterfalls, and peaks
- You don't need to drive the full thing — go as far as you want and turn back

**Key Stops (south to north):**

1. **Bow Lake** — Turquoise lake right off the highway, easy access
2. **Peyto Lake** — Short uphill walk to viewpoint; wolf-head-shaped lake, impossibly blue
3. **Mistaya Canyon** — Short trail to a narrow slot canyon with churning water
4. **Saskatchewan River Crossing** — Gas & food (last services for a while)
5. **Columbia Icefield / Athabasca Glacier**
   - Book the **Ice Explorer Tour** to ride onto the glacier ($$$, but unique)
   - OR walk the free Toe of the Glacier trail (1km, see the glacier retreating)
   - **Glacier Skywalk** — Glass-floored platform 280m above the valley

**Wildlife:** Watch for bears, mountain goats, bighorn sheep, elk along the road. Especially common near Bow Summit and Saskatchewan Crossing.

**Evening:** Return to Banff or Lake Louise

---

### June 25 (Day 4) — Johnston Canyon + Chill Day

**Morning:**
- **Johnston Canyon** — One of Banff's most popular hikes
  - Lower Falls: 1.1km, easy, paved/boardwalk, impressive waterfall in a narrow canyon
  - Upper Falls: 2.7km, moderate, bigger falls
  - **Ink Pots:** 5.8km total, flat meadow with turquoise bubbling spring pools (worth the extra push)

**Afternoon — Pick one:**
- **Vermilion Lakes** — Rent a canoe/kayak, paddle with Mt. Rundle as a backdrop
- **Legacy Trail** — Rent bikes and ride the paved path between Banff and Canmore (22km, flat, gorgeous)
- **Canmore exploration** — Less touristy than Banff, excellent coffee shops, restaurants, and local art galleries

**Evening:**
- Dinner in Canmore: The Iron Goat, Grizzly Paw Brewing, or Blake Brewhouse

---

### June 26 (Day 5) — Choose Your Adventure

Pick based on your energy level and interests:

**Option A: Sunshine Meadows (Alpine Hiking)**
- Take the shuttle/gondola up to the alpine zone
- Hike through meadows — wildflowers just starting to bloom in late June
- Views into BC, multiple alpine lakes
- Trails range from 3km to 12km+
- Above treeline the entire time — wide open panoramas

**Option B: Kananaskis Country**
- Less crowded alternative to Banff, just as beautiful
- **Grassi Lakes** (4km RT, easy/moderate) — Two stunning turquoise pools with cliff views
- **Rawson Lake** (8km RT, moderate) — Beautiful alpine lake beneath a wall of peaks
- **Spray Lakes** — Quiet, great for a picnic

**Option C: White Water Rafting**
- **Kicking Horse River** (2 hrs from Banff, in BC) — Class III–IV rapids, half-day or full-day trips
- Several outfitters run trips from Banff with transport included
- Thrilling with mountain scenery

**Option D: Lake Minnewanka & Two Jack Lake**
- **Lake Minnewanka boat cruise** — Scenic cruise on Banff's largest lake, mountain views
- **Two Jack Lake** — Quieter, great for kayaking or lounging on rocky beach
- Short hike to Stewart Canyon from Lake Minnewanka parking lot

## Part 6: Driving Through Yellowstone — Best Stops in One Pass

**Route through the park:** West Entrance → Madison → Old Faithful → West Thumb → South Entrance (into Grand Teton)

This route hits the highlights without backtracking, and takes you straight out the south end toward home.

**Stop 1: Grand Prismatic Spring & Midway Geyser Basin (~30–45 min)**
- Quick boardwalk right off the road
- The massive rainbow hot spring — largest in North America
- If you have an extra 20 min, do the Grand Prismatic Overlook Trail (1.6mi RT) for the aerial view

**Stop 2: Old Faithful (~45–60 min)**
- Check eruption prediction time at the visitor center (every ~90 min)
- Time your arrival so you don't wait too long
- Walk the short Upper Geyser Basin boardwalk while waiting — Morning Glory Pool is nearby

**Stop 3: West Thumb Geyser Basin (~20–30 min)**
- On Yellowstone Lake's edge, right on your route south
- Quick boardwalk loop with hot springs bubbling into the lake
- Good leg stretch before the final push

**Stop 4: Grand Teton — Oxbow Bend & Jackson Lake (~20–30 min)**
- As you exit Yellowstone into Grand Teton, pull over at Oxbow Bend for the classic Teton reflection shot
- Jackson Lake overlook is right off the road

**Optional if time allows:**
- **Hayden Valley** (between Old Faithful and West Thumb) — Drive slow, watch for bison herds, possibly bears
- **Jenny Lake** — Quick stop if you want to stretch your legs at a turquoise alpine lake (adds 20 min)

**Then continue south to Jackson, WY** for dinner/overnight, or push toward home if it's still early.

### Yellowstone Drive-Through — Practical Tips
- **Entrance fee:** $35/vehicle (7-day pass) covers both Yellowstone & Grand Teton, or use America the Beautiful Pass
- **Bison jams:** Budget extra time. Herds walk on the road and can cause 15–30+ min delays. Can't be avoided, just enjoy it.
- **Start early from Bozeman:** Enter the park by 8–9am to beat the worst traffic and parking issues at Old Faithful
- **Gas:** Fill up in West Yellowstone before entering. Next gas inside is at Old Faithful or Grant Village.
- **Food:** Pack lunch/snacks — park food is mediocre and overpriced
- **Cell service:** None inside the park. Download offline maps before entering.

## Reservations & Booking Guide (June 19–28, 2026)

### GREAT NEWS: Parks Canada is FREE this summer!

Parks Canada is offering **free admission** to all national parks from **June 19 to September 7, 2026** (the "Canada Strong Pass" program). This covers Banff, Waterton Lakes, and Kootenay — no Discovery Pass needed! You'll also get 25% off camping and overnight stays at Parks Canada campgrounds.

---

### What DOES Need a Reservation

| What | Required? | Where to Book | Notes |
|------|-----------|---------------|-------|
| Moraine Lake shuttle | **YES — required** | Parks Canada (reservation.pc.gc.ca) or Roam Transit (roamtransit.com) | Moraine Lake is NOT accessible by private vehicle. You must take a shuttle. Book ASAP — popular dates sell out. |
| Lake Louise shuttle (8X) | **Recommended** | Roam Transit (roamtransit.com) | ~40% of seats are unreserved walk-up, but wait can be 2+ hours. Reserving saves major headaches. |
| Columbia Icefield Ice Explorer | **Recommended** | banffjaspercollection.com | Walk-ups possible but can sell out on busy summer days. Book online to guarantee a time slot. |
| Banff Gondola | **Recommended** | banffjaspercollection.com | Booking online saves time and sometimes gets a small discount. Walk-ups available but longer waits. |
| Accommodations (Banff, Lake Louise, Glacier area) | **YES** | Various (Hotels.com, Booking.com, NPS lodges) | Late June is peak season. Book ASAP — especially in Banff townsite and near Glacier. |

---

### What Does NOT Need a Reservation

| What | Reservation? | Notes |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Going-to-the-Sun Road (Glacier NP) | **NO vehicle reservation in 2026** | The reservation system has been dropped for 2026. You can just drive in! |
| Logan Pass parking (Glacier) | **No reservation, BUT 3-hour parking limit starts July 1** | You're visiting before July 1 (June 19–20ish), so NO time limit applies to you. Just drive up and park. |
| Logan Pass Shuttle (Glacier) | **Not needed for your dates** | The ticketed shuttle starts July 1, 2026. Before that date, just drive and park at Logan Pass freely. |
| Yellowstone National Park | **NO reservation needed** | No vehicle reservation or timed entry for 2026. Just buy an entrance pass ($35/vehicle) at the gate or online. |
| Grand Teton National Park | **NO reservation needed** | Covered by your Yellowstone entrance pass. |
| Waterton Lakes National Park | **NO reservation for day use** | Free admission starting June 19. Just drive in. Only camping needs a reservation. |
| Banff National Park entry | **FREE — no pass needed** | Free admission June 19 – Sept 7, 2026 (Canada Strong Pass program). |
| Johnston Canyon, Peyto Lake, etc. | **No reservation** | Just show up. Arrive early for popular spots to get parking. |

### Reservation Action Items — Do These NOW

1. **Moraine Lake Shuttle** — BOOK IMMEDIATELY
   - Options: Parks Canada shuttle (reservation.pc.gc.ca, 1-877-737-3783) OR Roam Transit "Super Pass" (roamtransit.com) OR private shuttles like morainelakebus.com (~$51 USD RT) or viaviamorainelake.com
   - You cannot drive to Moraine Lake — shuttle is the only option
   - Parks Canada released 40% of seats on April 15, 2026; remaining seats release on a rolling basis

2. **Lake Louise Shuttle (Roam Transit 8X)** — Book at roamtransit.com
   - Reservable Super Pass gives you access to both Lake Louise AND Moraine Lake via the Lake Louise lakeshore connector
   - Walk-up is possible but risky in late June (2+ hour waits)

3. **Columbia Icefield Ice Explorer** — Book at banffjaspercollection.com
   - Pick your Icefields Parkway day and book a time slot
   - Tours run 9am–5pm during your dates

4. **Accommodations** — Book all lodging now for:
   - Glacier area (West Glacier, East Glacier, or St. Mary) — 2 nights
   - Banff / Canmore — 5 nights
   - Bozeman, MT (drive home overnight) — 1 night
   - Jackson, WY or Rock Springs (post-Yellowstone) — 1 night if needed

5. **Glacier NP entrance pass** — $35/vehicle at the gate or buy at recreation.gov beforehand to skip the line

6. **Yellowstone entrance pass** — $35/vehicle, buy at recreation.gov or at the gate

### Important Note on Going-to-the-Sun Road Timing

Going-to-the-Sun Road typically opens fully (over Logan Pass) between **June 16–25** depending on snowfall. Since you're arriving around June 19–20:
- There's a **good chance** the road is fully open, but it's not guaranteed
- Check the status a few days before at: nps.gov/glac/planyourvisit/roadstatus.htm
- If it's not yet open over Logan Pass, you can still enjoy the lower sections from both sides (beautiful drives to Avalanche Creek from the west, and to Sun Point/St. Mary Falls from the east)
- The 2025–2026 winter was relatively mild, which is a good sign for an earlier opening
